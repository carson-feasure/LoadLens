# LoadLens two-minute demo

1. Start on **Your semester at a glance**. Point out the four derived summaries: four courses / 16 units, 16 average hours per week, the 23-hour Week 7 peak, and three weeks over the 20-hour study limit. Select Week 7 in the chart and show that the course breakdown adds to 23 hours.

2. Switch the schedule card between **Day**, **Week**, and **Semester**. On Day, choose a day without a sample deadline to show the honest empty state. Explain that due dates are deadline markers while chart hours represent work distributed through the week.

3. Open **Build / Edit my semester**. Search `GEOL107` or use **Add course**. Show that **Your courses** is explicitly a draft, then choose **Analyze my workload** to commit it. Use **Reset draft to current plan** instead if you do not want to change the default before comparison.

4. On **Your Fall Semester**, select Week 7. Expand **How this adds up**: COG 107 is 6 h, WRIT 150 is 8 h, PSYC 100 is 5 h, and HIST 170 is 4 h, totaling 23 h. Use **View all weeks** for the complete accessible table.

5. Choose **Adjust my courses**. The prepared example is labeled `WRIT 150 → ISE 105` without claiming equivalence. Compare 16 versus 16 units, 23 versus 17 peak hours, three versus zero above-limit weeks, and 240 versus 194 semester hours. Demonstrate **Keep current** or **Use new schedule**; either outcome is valid.

6. Open the menu and choose **Prototype study**. Pick an initial plan before workload is shown, inspect the equal-scale charts, identify demanding weeks, then provide a final plan and reason. Confirm the response is saved only on this browser and does not modify the main plan.

7. If needed, use **Reset demo** from the menu to restore the exact seeded Plan A after confirmation.
